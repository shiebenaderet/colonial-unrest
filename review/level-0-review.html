Proclamation of 1763 1763

The French and Indian War ended in 1763. Britain won. After the war, Britain claimed the land west of the Appalachian Mountains.

That year, the king of Britain made a new rule. It was called the Proclamation of 1763. A proclamation is an official order. The rule said colonists could not settle west of the mountains. Native American nations already lived on that land. The king said colonists had to stay out.

Britain made the rule because it wanted to stop more fighting with Native American nations. But many colonists were angry. They wanted to move west to get good, cheap farmland. Now they could not. The rule also showed that Britain wanted more control over the colonies.

Quartering Act 1765

To quarter soldiers means to give them a place to stay. In 1765, Parliament passed the Quartering Act. Parliament is the group that makes laws for Britain. This law said the colonies had to give British soldiers places to stay. The soldiers stayed in barracks, inns, and empty buildings. Barracks are buildings where soldiers live. The law did not make families keep soldiers in their homes. The colonies also had to give the soldiers fuel, candles, and rides from place to place. The colonies had to pay for all of this.

Many colonists were angry. They did not trust the soldiers. They thought the soldiers came to control them, not to protect them. Colonists also felt that Britain should have asked them first.

Stamp Act 1765

After the French and Indian War, Britain owed a lot of money. British leaders thought the colonists should help pay for their own protection. So in 1765, Parliament passed the Stamp Act. Parliament is the group that makes laws for Britain. The Stamp Act was a tax on paper things, like newspapers and playing cards. A tax is money people must pay to the government. Colonists had to buy a stamp to show they paid.

The colonists were angry because they could not vote for anyone in Parliament. No one there spoke for them. They called this taxation without representation.

Colonists fought back in many ways. The Sons of Liberty scared the stamp sellers into quitting. Merchants (people who sell goods) started a boycott. A boycott is when people agree together not to buy something, to protest. They would not buy British goods. Leaders from nine colonies met. This meeting was called the Stamp Act Congress. They asked Britain to repeal the law. Repeal means to cancel a law.

In 1766, Parliament did repeal the Stamp Act. But on the same day, Parliament passed another law. It said Parliament could still make laws for the colonies. So the anger did not go away.

Townshend Acts 1767

In 1767, Parliament passed new taxes called the Townshend Acts. A British leader named Charles Townshend planned them. They were taxes on glass, lead, paint, paper, and tea. The taxes were paid at the ports, when ships came in. So the tax was hidden inside the price at the store. Townshend hoped colonists would not be as upset.

But colonists noticed the taxes. They still could not vote for anyone in Parliament. So again they called it taxation without representation. To protest, colonists started another boycott. They would not buy British goods.

In 1770, Parliament repealed most of these taxes. Repeal means to cancel a law. But Parliament kept the tax on tea.

Boston Massacre 1770

On March 5, 1770, a crowd in Boston yelled at British soldiers. Some people threw snowballs at them. Things got out of control. The soldiers fired their guns into the crowd. Five colonists died. People called this the Boston Massacre.

Most colonists blamed the soldiers. The people of Boston held a town meeting. They wanted the soldiers to leave Boston. They wanted the soldiers to go on trial for murder. The governor of Massachusetts agreed. The soldiers moved out of the town. Later, the soldiers went on trial.

Paul Revere printed a famous picture of the event. It spread fast, so many colonists got even angrier. But the picture did not show the whole story. The real night was more confusing than the picture. John Adams was the soldiers' lawyer in court. Not everyone agreed about who was to blame.

Пол Ревир (Paul Revere) напечатал известный рисунок об этом событии. Рисунок быстро разошёлся, и многие колонисты рассердились ещё сильнее. Но рисунок показывал не всю историю. На самом деле в ту ночь всё было запутаннее, чем на рисунке. Джон Адамс (John Adams) был адвокатом солдат в суде. Люди спорили о том, кто виноват.

Boston Tea Party 1773

In 1773, Parliament passed the Tea Act. The law helped one British company, the East India Company. It let the company sell tea very cheaply. That hurt colonial sellers, because they could not sell tea for such a low price.

The tea was cheaper. But it still had a tax on it. The colonists did not vote for that tax. If they bought the tea, it would be like saying Parliament could tax them. So this was taxation without representation again.

To protest, the Sons of Liberty planned the Boston Tea Party. Some colonists dressed up like Mohawk Indians. That way, no one would know who they were. They climbed onto three ships that carried the company's tea. They dumped 342 chests of tea into Boston Harbor. A chest is a big wooden box. A big crowd watched.

Intolerable (Coercive) Acts 1774

To coerce means to force someone to do something. After the Boston Tea Party, Britain wanted to punish Boston. So in 1774, Parliament passed the Coercive Acts. These laws closed the port of Boston, so ships could not come in or go out. The port would stay closed until Boston paid for the tea. The king also put an army general, Thomas Gage, in charge of Massachusetts. More soldiers came to Boston. The laws also limited town meetings.

Colonists were shocked. They called the laws the Intolerable Acts. Intolerable means too harsh to accept. The laws only punished Massachusetts. But the other colonies were worried, because they could be next. So they joined the protest. Some colonies sent food to Boston.

Near Boston, minutemen got ready to fight. Minutemen were colonists who could be ready to fight very fast, "at a minute's notice." They stored guns and gunpowder, and they trained for battle.

First Continental Congress 1774

After the Intolerable Acts, the colonies decided to work together. They knew they were stronger together than alone. They wanted to tell Britain their complaints as one group.

In 1774, leaders from 12 of the 13 colonies met in Philadelphia. This meeting was called the First Continental Congress. A congress is a meeting of people chosen to speak for others. Samuel Adams, John Adams, George Washington, and Patrick Henry were there.

They met for about seven weeks. They wrote down the rights they believed colonists had. They sent a letter to the king. They also agreed to a big boycott. First, colonists would stop buying British goods. Later, if Britain did not listen, they would also stop selling goods to Britain. Local groups made sure people obeyed the boycott.

Lexington & Concord 1775

Britain did not give the First Continental Congress what it asked for. In April 1775, British soldiers marched out of Boston. They went to the towns of Lexington and Concord. They wanted to take the colonists' guns and gunpowder. Colonists feared the soldiers also wanted to catch two leaders, Samuel Adams and John Hancock. Paul Revere rode at night to warn them.

At Lexington, minutemen met the British soldiers. Someone fired a shot, but no one knows who fired first. Eight colonists were killed. Then the British marched on to Concord. More minutemen fought them there.

The British turned back to Boston. On the way, colonists shot at them from behind trees and fences. By night, many more British soldiers than colonists were hurt or killed.

In June 1775, the Second Continental Congress chose George Washington to lead the colonists' army. The Revolutionary War had begun.
