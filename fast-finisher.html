★Easier

Protest Sign

Paper and colored pencils · 20 minutes

Open

Make a protest sign a colonist could have carried. Use one sheet of 8.5 x 11 paper.

  1. Pick one complaint from the list below.
  2. Write a short slogan on your sign (5 to 8 words).
  3. Draw a picture that shows the complaint. Fill the page.
  4. On the back, write your name, the event, and one sentence: "Colonists were angry because ___."

Complaints From the Placards

  • Proclamation of 1763We fought for that land, and now we can't settle on it.
  • Quartering ActWe have to house and supply soldiers we never asked for.
  • Stamp ActWe pay a tax on paper, but no one in Parliament speaks for us.
  • Townshend ActsGlass, paint, paper and tea are taxed before they reach our stores.
  • Boston MassacreSoldiers fired on a crowd in our own streets.
  • Tea Act (Boston Tea Party)Britain decided about our tea without asking us.
  • Intolerable ActsBoston's port is closed and our town meetings are limited.

Turn your sign in to Mr. B or put it in your class folder. Mr. B may hang the best ones on the wall.

★★★Hard

Engraving Detective

Primary source · 20 minutes

Open

Paul Revere made a famous picture of the Boston Massacre in 1770. It spread across the colonies and made people furious. But was it true? First look closely at the picture. Then check it against what witnesses said at the trials.

Part 1: Look Closely

Answer from the picture only. Each question shows you the part to look at.

The Evidence

What witnesses and the trials said about March 5, 1770.

  1. Evidence 1. The crowd threw snowballs, chunks of ice and sticks at the soldiers. People shouted at them and dared them to fire. Witnesses at the trials, 1770
  2. Evidence 2. One soldier was knocked down. The shots came one after another, in confusion, not all at once. Witnesses at the trials, 1770
  3. Evidence 3. Captain Preston, the officer, said he never gave an order to fire. A Boston jury found him not guilty. Preston’s trial, October 1770
  4. Evidence 4. Four men from the Custom House were put on trial for shooting from its windows. The jury found them not guilty. Trial, December 1770
  5. Evidence 5. Five men died: Samuel Gray, Samuel Maverick, James Caldwell, Crispus Attucks and Patrick Carr. The caption under Revere’s picture

Part 2: Picture vs. Evidence

Fact means the evidence agrees. Exaggerated means Revere changed it to make the British look worse. Left Out means something true that the picture does not show. Each one tells you which evidence to check.

Did you know? John Adams, a Patriot lawyer, defended the soldiers in court. Six of the eight soldiers were found not guilty, and two were found guilty of manslaughter. Revere also copied his design from another Boston artist, Henry Pelham, who was angry about it.
