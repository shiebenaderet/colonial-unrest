★Easier

Protest Sign

Paper and colored pencils · 20 minutes

Open

Make a protest sign a colonist could have carried. Use one sheet of 8.5 x 11 paper.

  1. Pick one complaint from the list below.
  2. Write a short slogan on your sign (5 to 8 words).
  3. Draw a picture that shows the complaint. Fill the page.
  4. On the back, write your name, the event, and one sentence: "Colonists were angry because ___."

Complaints From the Placards

  • Proclamation of 1763We fought for that land, and now we can't settle on it.
  • Quartering ActWe have to house and supply soldiers we never asked for.
  • Stamp ActWe pay a tax on paper, but no one in Parliament speaks for us.
  • Townshend ActsGlass, paint, paper and tea are taxed before they reach our stores.
  • Boston MassacreSoldiers fired on a crowd in our own streets.
  • Tea Act (Boston Tea Party)Britain decided about our tea without asking us.
  • Intolerable ActsBoston's port is closed and our town meetings are limited.

Turn your sign in to the tray. Mr. B may hang the best ones on the wall.

★★★Hard

Engraving Detective

Primary source · 20 minutes

Open

Paul Revere made a famous picture of the Boston Massacre in 1770. It spread across the colonies and made people furious. But was it true? Open the picture and look closely. Then decide what each detail is.

Open the EngravingIt is the first picture on the page. Tap it to zoom in.

Fact means it really happened. Exaggerated means Revere changed it to make the British look worse. Left Out means something true that the picture hides.

★★★Hard

Boston 1770 Breakout

Four locks · 20 to 30 minutes

Open

The Sons of Liberty locked their secret papers in a chest. Open all four locks to find the password. Every student gets different codes, so you have to solve your own.

★★★★Challenge

Mission US: For Crown or Colony?

Game with headphones · 40 minutes or more

Open

Play Nat Wheeler, a 14-year-old printer's apprentice in Boston in 1770. You will meet Patriots and Loyalists, and your choices change what happens to Nat. This one is long, so it's for students who finish early more than once.

Play Mission USFree, no account needed for guest play.

Choice Log

Write down three choices Nat made. For each one, say which side it pushed him toward: Patriot, Loyalist, or neither.
