# Slides brief: Unrest in the Colonies

For updating the Unit 2 slides for Mon Oct 12 to Wed Oct 14. Everything below
is live at **https://unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org** as of Oct 10, 2026. The
Canvas assignment page is `canvas/unit2-unrest-in-the-colonies.html`, the
printed tracker is `canvas/unrest-tracker.pdf`, and the printable readings are
`canvas/unrest-readings.pdf` (also live at `/readings.html`).

## 1. What the activity is, in one slide

- Nine events, 1763 to 1775, one placard each: Proclamation of 1763,
  Quartering Act, Stamp Act, Townshend Acts, Boston Massacre, Boston Tea
  Party, Intolerable Acts, First Continental Congress, Lexington & Concord.
- Students read each one at their level, rate the unrest it caused from 1 to 8,
  and write why. Then, with a partner, they spend exactly 36 points across the
  nine events. Then they answer three reflection questions and turn in on
  Canvas.
- The unit question it builds toward: *How did loyal British subjects turn
  into revolutionaries in barely a decade?*
- Outcomes: H2.6-8.5 (analyzing how people and movements changed history) and
  H1.6-8.6 (explaining what makes an era an era). The app quotes both verbatim
  under "What we are learning" on placard 1.

## 2. The schedule (three days, no "Day N" numbers on student materials)

| When | What | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Mon Oct 12 | Model placard 1 together, then students sign in and rate two or three placards. End with **Save a backup**. | full period |
| Tue Oct 13 | Students finish all nine placards. | full period |
| Wed Oct 14 | Post partners with pair numbers. Pairs agree on 36 points (15 min). You type each pair's numbers into class.html and discuss the chart (10 min). Students reflect and turn in (20 min). | full period |

Say "partner" and "pair," never "group" or "table group." The app's field is
called **Pair number**.

## 3. The unrest scale (one slide, show it exactly)

Prompt: **Imagine you live in the colonies. How angry would this make people,
and how many of them? Pick the number that fits.**

| # | Name | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Barely noticed | Almost nobody cares. Life goes on. |
| 2 | Grumbling | A few people are annoyed and say so to friends. |
| 3 | Upset | Many people are bothered. It comes up in town and in the papers. |
| 4 | Angry | Most people are angry, and they want Britain to hear it. |
| 5 | Protest | Anger is strong and shared. People are ready to act together. |
| 6 | Fury | Crowds are furious. Tempers are hot enough to turn rough. |
| 7 | Defiance | Whole colonies are angry together and no longer willing to obey. |
| 8 | Ready to fight | Anger is everywhere, and people will risk their lives over it. |

Why it changed: The old meanings named actions (boycott, petition, march, arm),
so a student could match the word "boycott" in the Stamp Act reading to a 5.
The new meanings measure **how angry and how many**. When you model placard 1,
say that out loud: "The number is not about what they did. It is about how
angry people were and how many of them."

The key opens automatically on placard 1 and is one tap away ("What do the
numbers mean?") on every later placard. The chosen number's meaning shows
under the squares.

## 4. Who pushed: The tags and the arrows (one slide)

Every placard shows a tag beside the title, on its own line after "Who acted:"

- **→ British Action** (Proclamation, Quartering, Stamp, Townshend, Intolerable)
- **← Colonial Response** (Tea Party, First Continental Congress)
- **→← Both Sides Clash** (Boston Massacre, Lexington & Concord)

On the paper tracker the label is *not* printed. Under each event is
"Colonists [box] Britain": students draw an arrow from the side that pushed,
or an X when both sides clashed. Tell them the app shows the answer and they
draw it on the tracker, so by the end they see the back-and-forth on paper. On Wednesday the class chart shows the
tags under each event, and the "Think deeper" prompt asks: after each British
action, what did colonists do, and how did Britain answer?

## 5. Modeling placard 1 (what to click, in order)

1. **Sign in**: first name, last initial, period, reading level. Each level
   card shows the same sentence at that level. Level can be changed on any
   placard with the Level 0 to 3 tabs.
2. **Listen**: there is now a headphones **Listen** button beside the level
   tabs on every placard. It reads the whole screen aloud and highlights each
   sentence. Level 0 can be read in Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, or Chinese
   from the "Read in" menu, and Listen reads it in that language.
3. **Rate**: tap a number 1 to 8. The meaning appears under the squares.
4. **Why**: the question is "Why did this event cause N out of 8 unrest?"
   Point out **Look at:** (where the evidence is in the reading) and the
   **Start with** chips. One chip on every placard asks for **Britain's
   side**, for example "Britain thought this would ___, but colonists saw it
   as ___ because ___." Model using it once so they know Britain's reasons
   count as evidence too. Level 0 adds a tap-to-insert word bank.
5. **Tracker**: write the same rating and a few words on paper. Draw who
   pushed in the box.
6. **Save & next**. At the end of class, **Save a backup** (top right). On a
   different Chromebook, **Load a backup** on the sign-in screen restores it.

## 6. Wednesday: 36 points with a partner

- Post the pairs on the board with a number beside each pair. Students type
  that number into the app's **Pair number** field and write it on the tracker
  ("wait for Mr. B's number").
- Each event needs at least 1 point; total must be exactly 36. The points bar
  at the bottom of the screen turns green and the **Continue to reflections**
  button grows and pulses when it balances. Before that it reads "Continue
  (spend all 36 first)."
- Pairs write the agreed points in **Our Pair's Points (1–8)** on the tracker.
- You open **https://unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org/class.html**, pick the
  period, and type each pair's nine numbers into the row with their pair
  number. The page shows the class average per event, a running-total line
  from 1763 to 1775 with who pushed under each event, the biggest jump, and
  which events pairs disagreed on most. **Present** mode is for projecting.
  Data stays in your browser, one set per period.

## 7. Reflection and turn-in (three questions)

1. Which event did you move the most points on, and what changed your mind?
   (Look at: The "How your ratings changed" table.)
2. Which event changed what happened next the most, and how? Two pickers:
   **This event…** and **…led to** (only later events offered). The picks fill
   the event names into the sentence starters. Hint: look at your two
   highest-rated events and the event right before each one. Which earlier
   event set it off?
3. The unit question, with the class chart as evidence: *How did loyal British
   subjects turn into revolutionaries in barely a decade?*

Turn in: **Copy my work** copies every rating, reason, the pair's points, and
the reflections. Students open the Canvas assignment **Unrest in the
Colonies**, Start Assignment, paste, Submit. One submission per person. The
app shows a Canvas logo beside the turn-in heading so they know where it goes.

## 8. Supports to mention once

- **Listen** on every placard; **Font** (Atkinson, Lexend, OpenDyslexic,
  Verdana, size, spacing); **Settings** has light/dark, "Shorter answers OK,"
  backups, and start over.
- Level 0 translations exist for the readings, key terms, the who-pushed tags,
  and the scale. **Caveat:** the translated scale meanings are still the older
  action-based wording until a native speaker checks the drafts in
  `TRANSLATIONS-TO-CHECK.md`. English readers see the new wording.
- Key terms sit under every reading. The five Unit 2 words are tagged
  **Unit 2 word**: Proclamation of 1763, taxation without representation,
  boycott, repeal, Intolerable Acts. There is no vocab card.
