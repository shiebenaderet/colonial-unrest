Interactive Activity

Unrest in the Colonies

Nine events, 1763 to 1775. Rate the unrest each one caused, then spend 36 points on the ones that mattered most.
⏰ Time
Three class periods, Mon Oct 12 to Wed Oct 14		 🤝 Work style
On your own to read and rate; with a partner for the 36 points		 📅 Due
Wednesday, Oct 14, end of class		 🎯 Focus
Cause and effect: How anger built from 1763 to 1775

Your job: Imagine you live in the colonies. For each of nine events, decide how angry people were and how many of them, and explain why with evidence from the reading. Then, with your partner, decide which events mattered most. By Wednesday you will be able to answer the unit question: How did loyal British subjects turn into revolutionaries in barely a decade?

🎯 What we are learning

WALT: explain how a chain of events between 1763 and 1775 built the unrest that turned loyal colonists into revolutionaries.

✍🏾 Content Objectives

  • I can rate how much unrest each event caused and back up my rating with evidence from the reading.
  • I can explain how one event led to the next, on both the British and the colonial side.
  • I can use the class chart to explain where anger grew fastest.

💬 Language Objectives

  • I can write a reason in a full sentence using because, so, or led to.
  • I can use the five Unit 2 words: Proclamation of 1763, taxation without representation, boycott, repeal, Intolerable Acts.
  • I can write in direct voice, with no "I think" or "I believe."

📋 What to do

Part 1: Sign in and pick your level (Monday, 5 minutes)

  1. Open the activity (button below). Type your first name, last initial, and period.
  2. Pick a reading level. Each card shows the same sentence at that level, so pick the one you understand best. You can change it on any placard.
  3. Level 0 can be read in Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, or Chinese from the "Read in" menu. The Listen button reads any screen aloud.

Part 2: Rate the nine placards (Monday and Tuesday)

  1. Read the placard. Notice who pushed, beside the title: Colonists ← Britain (British Action), Colonists → Britain (Colonial Response), or Colonists →← Britain (Both Sides Clash). Draw it in the box on your tracker: an arrow from the side that pushed, or an X when both clashed.
  2. Rate the unrest from 1 to 8. Ask: How angry would this make people, and how many of them? Open "What do the numbers mean?" if you need the key.
  3. Write why in a full sentence. Use the "Start with" sentence starters. Point to the part of the reading that shows how people reacted.
  4. Write the same rating and a few words on your paper tracker. It is your backup if a computer resets.
  5. Before you leave on Monday, press Save a backup. On Tuesday, finish all nine.

Part 3: Spend 36 points with your partner (Wednesday, 15 minutes)

  1. Find your partner and your pair number on the board. Type the pair number into the app.
  2. Together, agree on how many points each event gets. They must add up to exactly 36, so you cannot give every event an 8. Decide which events mattered most.
  3. Write the agreed points in the "Our pair" column of your tracker. I will build the class chart from every pair's numbers.

Part 4: Reflect and turn in (Wednesday, 20 minutes)

  1. Answer the three reflection questions. The last one is the unit question, and the class chart is your evidence.
  2. Press Copy my work, come back to this assignment, press Start Assignment, paste, and Submit.

💭 Words you will need

Word What it means here
Proclamation of 1763A British order banning colonial settlement west of the Appalachian Mountains after the French and Indian War.
Taxation without RepresentationBeing taxed by a government in which you have no elected voice.
BoycottAn organized refusal to buy certain goods as a form of protest.
RepealTo officially cancel a law.
Intolerable ActsThe 1774 laws punishing Massachusetts for the Boston Tea Party, which Britain called the Coercive Acts.
UnrestRising anger and pressure from actions people see as unfair. The 1 to 8 scale measures how angry people were and how many of them.
