# Understanding Unrest in the Colonies

An interactive classroom activity for 8th grade U.S. History (Unit 2, Days 4–6). Students read nine events on the road to the American Revolution (1763–1775) at one of four reading levels (Quick, Clear, Full, Deep), rate how much unrest each created, spend a fixed 36-point "outrage budget," reflect, and turn in their work on Canvas.

## Using it

Students use it at **https://unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org** (GitHub Pages, set by the `CNAME` file; the old `shiebenaderet.github.io/colonial-unrest/` link redirects there). You can also open `index.html` directly in any modern browser. There is no build step. The page is `index.html` plus `reader-tools.js`, `fonts/`, and `images/`. Progress autosaves in the browser (localStorage), so students can close the tab and pick up again on the same device.

### Activity flow
1. **Sign in.** First name, last initial, period, and a reading (each card shows the same sentence in Quick, Clear, Full, and Deep). Plain words only; no standards on this screen.
2. **Placards (Days 4–5).** One screen per event: The reading and its picture on the left, "Your turn" on the right (rate 1–8, then write why). Each placard carries a who-acted pill under the title, with the arrow drawn from the side that pushed, the same way round as the tracker: **Colonists ← Britain** (British Action), **Colonists → Britain** (Colonial Response), or **Colonists →← Britain** (Both Sides Clash: the Boston Massacre and Lexington & Concord). The back-and-forth that built unrest stays visible. The tag is translated for students reading Quick in another language. Placard 1 opens a scale key above the 1–8 squares (*Imagine you live in the colonies. How angry would this make people, and how many of them? Pick the number that fits.*): 1 Barely noticed, 2 Grumbling, 3 Upset, 4 Angry, 5 Protest, 6 Fury, 7 Defiance, 8 Ready to fight, each described by how angry people were and how many, never by which action they took (so a word like "boycott" in a reading cannot be matched to a number). The Quick translations of the key still carry the older action-based meanings until the drafts in `TRANSLATIONS-TO-CHECK.md` are checked by a native speaker. On later placards it is one tap away, and the chosen number's meaning shows under the squares. The tracker prints the same key. Progress dots across the top. The learning goals (quoted verbatim from Canvas: H2.6-8.5 and H1.6-8.6, levels 3 and 4) sit in a collapsed "What we are learning" panel on placard 1.
3. **Spend 36 points with your partner (Day 6).** You post partners, each pair with a pair number. Pairs agree on one 36-point budget on their trackers ("Our pair" column); each student enters it in the app as the Pair number. Students get the number from you: post the pairs with a number beside each, and that number is the row you type their budget into on class.html.
4. **Class average (Day 6).** You type each pair's numbers into **class.html** (`unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org/class.html`): it charts the class average five ways (Present mode for projecting): **Each event** (bars colored by who acted, with the lowest-to-highest pair and an even-split line at 4), **Push and push back** (each event's bar on the side that acted, Britain left and colonists right, with what each side did next written in the gap between events, so students can watch each answer get stronger), **Getting worse?** (1763–1767 against 1770–1775, the escalation answer), **Step by step** (how much more or less unrest each event caused than the one before), and **Adding up** (the running total to 36). Beside the chart are the before-and-after-1770 averages, the top events, the biggest step up, and the events pairs disagreed on most. **Save this chart** / **Save all views** download PNGs for slides or Canvas, **Export as slides** downloads a PowerPoint deck (it opens in Google Slides too: File → Import slides, or upload it to Drive) with a title slide, where the numbers come from and the who-acted colors, one slide per chart with *How to read it* steps in plain words and a *Talk about it* question built from the class's numbers, teacher notes under each chart slide, and a closing slide back to the essential question, and **Save a backup** / **Load a backup** move every period's numbers to another computer. The Day 6 "Think deeper" list asks students to follow the tags: after each British Action, what did colonists do, and how did Britain answer? Data stays in your browser, one set per period. Pick **All periods** from the Show menu to pool every period's pairs into one chart (each pair counts the same; type numbers in a single period).
5. **Reflect and turn in.** Three questions. The second (*Which event changed what happened next the most, and how?*) has two pickers, the event and what it led to (only later events are offered); the picks fill the event names into the sentence starters and are listed with the copied work. The third is the Unit 2 essential question, verbatim: *How did loyal British subjects turn into revolutionaries in barely a decade?*, with the class chart as evidence. Above it, **Use our class data** shows the whole grade's Day 6 averages (all periods) as a bar list in the who-acted colors, links to the class slides, and walks students through using the numbers: did unrest grow (before and after 1770), what Britain did, how colonists answered, and what happened when the two sides clashed. The numbers and the slides link live in `CLASS_DATA` in `class-data.js`; update them after a new class average (the help page reads the same file). For Level 0 readers in Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, or Chinese, the question, its starters (as a line under each English starter) and the class-data box show in that language, with *Show this in English* (drafts, listed in `TRANSLATIONS-TO-CHECK.md`). Under each question's sentence starters is a **Step-by-Step Help** box (shorter steps for Quick readers) with a sample answer behind a second click; sample answers can be read but not copied or clicked into the answer box. The same steps are on a page you can link students to: **unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org/reflect-help.html** (`#q1`, `#q2`, `#q3` jump to each question), which adds the four class chart images from the Day 6 slides (`images/class-data/`, a snapshot of the Oct 14 numbers: re-save them from class.html after a new class average) and the slides link. The wording for both lives in `reflect-help.js`. The help is English only for now. The reflection questions no longer show a **Look at** line. Students press **Copy my work** and paste into the Canvas text-entry assignment **Unrest in the Colonies** (it includes every rating, reason, the group's points and the reflections).

### Backups
Work saves in the browser on that computer. **Save a backup** (placard screens and Settings) downloads a small file of everything; **Load a backup** (sign-in screen and Settings) restores it on any computer. The browser asks before closing if there is work that hasn't been backed up. Starting over takes two steps: a warning that offers a backup, then typing RESTART.

**Set up in Canvas:** create a text-entry assignment named *Unrest in the Colonies*, so students can find it by that name.

### Reading levels

Reading levels are named Quick, Clear, Full, Deep (formerly Level 0–3). What each level means, and the measured limits every site keeps, is in [READING-LEVELS.md](READING-LEVELS.md); `node tools/check-levels.js` checks the labels, blurbs and how hard each placard's text measures. The names describe the text, never the reader. Only the labels changed: the data keys (`l0`, `ss`, `core`, `adv`), saved work, and Mr. B's recordings are untouched, so work saved before the rename still loads.

| Reading | The text | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Quick** | About grade 2–3. Short sentences, with key words explained in the text. | Can be read in **Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, or Simplified Chinese** from the "Read in" menu. Key unit terms keep the English word beside the translation, and "Show this in English" sits under every translation. |
| **Clear** | Below grade (FK about 6). Plain words. | |
| **Full** | At grade (FK about 9). | Default if no reading is picked. |
| **Deep** | Above grade. Primary sources, cited, and historiography. | |

All levels carry the same facts. Quick and its translations were fact-checked against Clear, Full, and Deep and the historical record, and each translation was checked sentence by sentence against the English. Have a native speaker spot-check the translations when you can.

### Writing supports by level
Every placard question has one direct prompt, a **Look at** (where to find the evidence; the reflection questions use Step-by-Step Help instead), and **Start with** sentence starters keyed to the reading the student picked. On every placard, at every level, one starter asks for Britain's side and follows the who-acted tag: what Britain hoped a British Action would do, how Britain read a colonial response, or how each side saw a clash. Quick adds a tap-to-insert **word bank** and accepts shorter answers. Deep adds a **Going further** prompt aimed at the level-4 criterion.

### Paper backup
`tracker.html` prints one full-page tracker per letter sheet. Every level uses the same sheet: name, period, partner and pair number; the reading used (Quick · Clear · Full · Deep, circled once at the top); then for each event, who pushed (the student draws an arrow or an X in a box between Colonists and Britain), my rating, a ruled cell for why, our pair's points, and the class average. It is the paper record for the Day 6 pair budget and a backup if a computer resets.

## Suggested pacing

The Blueprint plans three days (Mon Oct 12 through Wed Oct 14). Rating alone takes most students two full periods.

- **Day 4 (Mon Oct 12):** Model one placard, then students sign in and rate two or three placards. Remind them to save a backup at the end of class.
- **Day 5 (Tue Oct 13):** Students finish rating the placards.
- **Day 6 (Wed Oct 14):** Post partners and pair numbers. Pairs agree on 36 points (about 15 min), you enter pair budgets on class.html and discuss the chart (about 10 min), then students reflect and turn in on Canvas.

Progress is saved per device. A student who switches Chromebooks loads their backup file; the Canvas submission and the paper tracker are the durable records.

## Accessibility and language support

- **Settings** (header) holds light/dark, "Shorter answers OK", Translate, backups, and start over.
- **Listen** (header) reads the current screen aloud and highlights each sentence. A Quick translation is read in its own language. You can tap any paragraph to jump there and change the speed. This comes from the course readings' `reader-tools.js` (see the header of `reader-tools.js` for what was adapted).
- **Font** (header) changes the reading area: it offers Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (the default, bundled), Lexend, OpenDyslexic, and Verdana, plus text size and wider spacing.
- **"Shorter answers OK"** (Settings) relaxes the full-sentence and punctuation rule.
- **Key terms** appear on every placard. The five Unit 2 terms (Proclamation of 1763, taxation without representation, boycott, repeal, Intolerable Acts) are word for word and tagged "Unit 2 word." Spanish, Portuguese, and French cognates are flagged.
- **Screen readers** get the point total, status messages, and a data table of the chart. The rating control and timeline work fully from the keyboard.
- **Reduced motion** is respected.

## Recording the readings (Mr. B's voice)

`record.html` (not linked anywhere, hidden from search engines) records the 36 English readings, 9 placards × 4 levels, for a **Mr. B** voice in Listen. Only `benaderets885@edmonds.wednet.edu` can sign in with Google. The page shows the real placard. Press **Record**, read the highlighted sentence, and press **Space** at the end of each one. Those taps time the highlight, so it follows your voice when students play it back. Listen back, keep the take or redo it. Takes stay in that browser until **Download all** saves them as one zip. `python3 tools/add-recordings.py <zip>` turns the zip into `audio/*.mp3` and `audio/recordings.js`. Readings without a recording, the translations, and any reading whose text changed after it was recorded keep the computer voice.

Sign-in needs a Google OAuth client ID (Web application, authorized JavaScript origin `https://unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org`) pasted into `CLIENT_ID` in `record.html`. The sign-in only keeps the page private. Nothing on it can change the site.

## Printable readings

`readings.html` (`unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org/readings.html`) is every reading at all four levels, plus Quick in each language, with a Print button. `canvas/unrest-readings.pdf` is the same page as a PDF. ## Common Sense, annotated (extra credit)

`common-sense.html` (**unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org/common-sense.html**) is Thomas Paine's *Common Sense* with help built in. The landing page has the Paine portrait and the 1776 title page, a few quick facts, and the reading picker (Quick, Clear, Full, Deep, with the standard blurbs). Text options: show or hide the plain-English side (hide it for a challenge), word help on or off, and light or dark. Listen and Font sit in the header, from `reader-tools.js`. Under the picker, **All 19 Passages** lists every passage by part, for opening one on the projector.

- **Reading page.** Paine's words on the left two-thirds (in Caslon, or in whatever face the student picks under Font), plain English at the student's reading on the right third, stacked on phones. Underlined words open a definition on tap. Full and Deep add a **Look Closer** note. Each passage ends with a **Stop and Think** question: Quick gets a fill-in frame and word bank; Clear, Full and Deep answer on their own (no sentence starters above Quick, Mr. B's rule); Deep adds an optional **Going Further** prompt.
- **How much each reading sees.** Quick: 4 single passages. Clear: 7. Full: 12 longer chunks. Deep: all 19 at full length, plus **common-sense-full.html**, the whole pamphlet with every passage shaded and linked back. Passages are trimmed with "…" where a reading skips Paine's words.
- **Finish Up.** Two reflections, one extension (Paine in a Post, The Loyalist Writes Back, Then and Now, A New Cover for Teens), then **Send to Mr. B** (same Apps Script as the Fast Finisher Menu; the email subject starts "Fast Finisher · Common Sense") or **Copy My Work**. Answers save in the browser as students type.
- **Direct links to one passage.** `common-sense.html#<id>` opens one passage on its own, with a reading switcher and its own Send button. Add `?level=quick|clear|full|deep` to set the reading: `common-sense.html?level=clear#ass-lion`. Every passage card has a **Copy Link** button. Ids, in order: `cause`, `society`, `tree`, `simple`, `ass-lion`, `ruffian`, `honest-man`, `sun`, `milk`, `parent`, `time-to-part`, `island`, `house-burnt`, `law-is-king`, `o-ye`, `unity`, `seed-time`, `putting-off`, `begin-again`. `?level=` also works on the main page (`common-sense.html?level=quick#read`).
- **Content** lives in `common-sense-data.js` (passages, plain English, notes, questions, vocabulary). `node tools/check-common-sense.js` measures every plain-English version against READING-LEVELS.md. After editing, rerun `python3 tools/build-common-sense-full.py` so the whole-pamphlet page links still match.

`common-sense-quiz.html` (**unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org/common-sense-quiz.html**), *Do You Have Common Sense?*, is the whole-class opener: 10 agree/disagree statements (for example, "A person should get to be a leader because of who their parents are"), then a Paine-o-meter score from Loyalist to Pure Common Sense and a "You vs. Paine" list with Paine's own words and a link to each passage. At the end, a student picks their period and can send the result (no name, only period and the 10 answers) to the mailer script, which writes one row per browser to the Google Sheet "Common Sense Quiz Results" (the sheet's Summary tab charts it by period; `setupQuizSheet` in `tools/fast-finisher-mailer.gs` builds it). The Fast Finisher Menu links to both pages as its three-star entry.

`common-sense-results.html` (**unrest.mrbsocialstudies.org/common-sense-results.html**) is the projector page for those results: it reads the sheet's Responses tab live (the sheet is shared read-only by link; the sheet ID is in the page), with tabs for each period and all classes, the class Paine-o-meter average, how many landed in each tier, and each statement's answers next to Paine's side. It refreshes every 20 seconds. Students never need it; it is not linked from the student pages.
